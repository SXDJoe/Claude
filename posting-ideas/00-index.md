## Sequence

Page 1 asks for topics sequenced so they tell one story. The list on page 2 has no sequence. This is the one the material supports.

1. Frontier transformation. Transformation programmes now behave like systems nobody can predict, and they are approved anyway.
2. Decision governance. The reason is not delivery. It is that the decisions have no owners, and adding forums makes it worse.
3. Governance paradox. The controls that protect the business are the controls that prevent it from changing, which is why approval and delivery keep diverging.
4. The offer. What a named buyer does differently after one engagement.

Posts one to three each follow the page 1 shape: name the failure in the opening lines, name the mechanism in the middle, close on what you would have done.

## Assessment of the set

The three-page sample contains one argument, one strong commercial thread, one premature decision, and two items that should not be posted as written.

The set has a single structural problem. Six of the seven items describe how organisations fail. None of them, in the note, says what a buyer gets. File 06 is where you asked that question yourself and left it open. Until it is answered, this sequence produces a good audience and no pipeline.

The second problem is definitional. Two of the three lead topics (frontier transformation, decision governance) are terms you have not defined. Undefined terms get defined by the comment section, and the definition that wins will be the conventional one you were trying to displace.
