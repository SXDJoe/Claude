# Frontier transformation

Source: `Posting Ideas.pdf`, page 2. One line: "frontier transformation, derived from Frontier AI model".
Status: label without a definition.

## What the note says

A term, and where it came from. Nothing else. The concept is borrowed from the frontier AI model idea and moved onto transformation.

## Reading

The transferable part of the frontier model is the operating condition, not the technology: capability arrives faster than the controls around it, the people deploying it cannot fully predict its behaviour, and the governing body is asked to approve something it does not understand. That condition now describes large transformation programmes, with or without AI in them.

## Evaluation

The idea is sound and the name is a liability.

"Frontier" is vendor vocabulary. A reader who sees it expects an AI post. When the post turns out to be about transformation governance, the reader feels switched, and a switched reader does not respond. The brief on page 1 asks for a response as the test. This label works against that test.

Second problem: a borrowed term has to earn its transfer. If the post cannot state in one sentence what specifically carries over from frontier AI to transformation, the term is decoration. Right now the note cannot, which is why it sits there as four words.

There is no case attached. Without one lived example this is a definition post, and definition posts do not trigger interaction.

## Angle if posted

The unpopular version: most transformation programmes are already frontier systems. Nobody in the steering committee can predict what the programme will do to the business, the controls were designed for a slower system, and approval is given anyway. The failure is not that the technology is new. It is that the governance model assumes predictability that stopped existing.

## What it needs before drafting

One sentence defining frontier transformation without using the word AI. One programme where the prediction failure was visible. A decision on whether to keep the borrowed name or drop it and keep the concept.
