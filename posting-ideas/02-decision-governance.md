# Decision governance

Source: `Posting Ideas.pdf`, page 2. "decision governance: explain, find definition".
Status: undefined by your own note.

## What the note says

Two instructions to yourself: explain it, and find a definition for it. You wrote the second because you know the first is not yet possible.

## Reading

Decision governance is the layer between corporate governance (who is accountable) and project governance (who runs what): which decisions exist, who holds each one, what evidence a decision requires, and what happens to a decision that nobody makes. Most transformation failures I would expect you to cite are failures in that layer, not in delivery.

## Evaluation

Commercially this is the strongest item on page 2. It names the thing a buyer already feels and has no words for. A buyer who cannot name a problem cannot buy a solution to it, and naming it is the service.

It is also the item with the most competition. Every consultancy sells governance. The word is worn out and the reader's default assumption is more process, more committees, more documents. The post has to defeat that assumption in the first two lines or it dies there.

The note is honest about the gap. Undefined means unsellable. This cannot go to a post before the definition exists, because the comment section will define it for you and it will be defined as "steering committee".

Do not let it become a taxonomy post. A list of decision types is correct and inert. The definition has to arrive through one decision that nobody owned and what it cost.

## Angle if posted

The unpopular version: adding governance is why decisions stop. Most organisations respond to a stalled decision by adding a forum, and the new forum gives everyone a place to defer to. Governance that works removes decision rights from groups and gives them to named people who can be wrong in public.

## What it needs before drafting

A definition in one sentence that a CFO would repeat. One decision, from real work, that had no owner, plus what the delay cost in time or money. The distinction from project governance, stated in a line.
